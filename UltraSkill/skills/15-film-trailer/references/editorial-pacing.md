# Editorial Pacing: Cutting on Action, Eye-Trace & J/L Cuts

## 1. Minimum Readable Shot Durations (`24 fps` Starting Values)

The human eye requires more frames to parse a complex wide architectural shot than a high-contrast close-up silhouette:

| Shot Type | Minimum Hold Duration (`frames @ 24 fps`) | Minimum Hold (`seconds`) | Why Shorter Fails |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Complex Wide Establishing Shot** | `60 – 96 frames` | `2.50 – 4.00 s` | Viewer needs time to scan horizon, architecture, and locate the hero subject. |
| **Medium Character / Vehicle Action** | `36 – 60 frames` | `1.50 – 2.50 s` | Allows one complete gesture or physical movement arc to read. |
| **Close-Up Reaction / Macro Prop** | `24 – 42 frames` | `1.00 – 1.75 s` | Single focal point (`f/2.8` shallow DOF) reads immediately. |
| **Act 3 Rhythmic Impact Flash** | `12 – 18 frames` | `0.50 – 0.75 s` | Only works if the subject sits at the exact screen `(X, Y)` coordinates of the preceding shot! |
| **Title Card / Text Slate** | `72 – 120 frames` | `3.00 – 5.00 s` | Must stay on screen long enough to read aloud twice at a calm speaking pace. |

## 2. Eye-Trace Matching Across Fast Cuts

During Act 3 montage sequences (`12–24 frames` per shot), never force the viewer's eyes to jump from the bottom-left corner of Shot A to the top-right corner of Shot B:
- Keep the primary high-contrast focal point within a **`15%` screen-width radius** across consecutive fast cuts (typically center-frame or upper-center `x = 0.50, y = 0.55`).

## 3. Cut on the Peak of Action (The 2-Frame Overlap Trick)

Never wait for an action in Shot A to finish and come to a dead stop before cutting to Shot B:
- Cut from the wide shot to the close-up **2 to 3 frames before** the hammer strikes the anvil, the door slams shut, or the character lands on the floor.
- The continuous motion momentum carries the viewer's brain across the edit invisibly.

## 4. J-Cuts and L-Cuts (Audio Leads Visual)

- **J-Cut (Audio Pre-Lap)**: Start the incoming environment sound, engine roar, or dialogue breath of Shot B **`6 to 18 frames` (`0.25 – 0.75 s`) before** the video cut occurs. This prepares the brain for the new location and makes the visual cut feel organic.
- **L-Cut (Audio Tail)**: Let the metallic reverb or impact echo of Shot A bleed **`12 to 36 frames` over** the incoming wide shot or blackout card.
